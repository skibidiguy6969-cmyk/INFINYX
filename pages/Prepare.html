Actionable Steps for Flood Readiness

Floods can happen unexpectedly. Having a concrete plan ensures you and your household know exactly what to do when heavy rainfall or an emergency alert is issued.

Based on global disaster management guidelines, stocking emergency supplies, securing your home utilities, and identifying evacuation routes are your primary defense.

Review Flood Risks First

By learning about flood risks and preparing early, we can make better decisions and help protect the people around us.

Ref: Ready.gov – Floods

5 Essential Preparation Steps

Follow these crucial guidelines to prepare your home and family before a flood strikes.

1. Emergency Kit

Gather non-perishable food, clean drinking water (1 gallon/person/day for at least 3 days), first-aid kit, prescription medications, flashlights, and extra batteries.

2. Family Plan

Establish family emergency communication channels, set up designated meeting spots, and assign an out-of-town contact person everyone can reach.

3. Secure Home

Move valuable electronics and essential documents to higher floors or waterproof containers. Clear gutters, rain spouts, and outdoor drains.

4. Monitor Weather Alerts

Stay informed via local weather apps, radio, or emergency news channels. Know the difference between a Flood Watch (possible) and a Flood Warning (imminent/occurring).

5. Evacuation Routes

Pre-plan multiple evacuation routes to higher ground. Keep your car's fuel tank full, and never drive through flooded roads or submerged bridges.

Recognize Early Flood Warning Signs

Be alert to these environmental signals so you can take quick safety action before flooding occurs.

Heavy Continuous Rain

Prolonged intense rain saturates soil rapidly, preventing drainage.

Rapid Water Elevation

Sudden rising of water levels in local canals, streams, or street gutters.

Muddy / Murky Runoff

Water turning muddy indicates intense soil erosion and upstream flash flooding.

Roaring Water Sounds

Hearing loud rushing water noises signals an approaching rush of floodwaters.

Do's & Don'ts Before a Flood

Critical actions to take and unsafe mistakes to avoid when severe weather threatens.

WHAT TO DO

  • Store clean drinking water in sealed containers (1 gal/person/day).
  • Elevate critical electrical appliances and household valuables.
  • Keep your phone fully charged and monitor local weather channels.
  • Shut off gas and main power if local authorities order evacuation.

WHAT NOT TO DO

  • Never walk, swim, or drive through moving floodwaters.
  • Do not touch electrical equipment if wet or standing in water.
  • Do not ignore official evacuation alerts or delay leaving safe areas.
  • Do not consume tap water or unsealed food affected by floodwater.
